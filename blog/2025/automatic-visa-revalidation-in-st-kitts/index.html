It was in April 2023 that I first learned about automatic visa revalidation. A little background. I am a final-year PhD student in computer science, and in 2023, I was just a second-year student moving into my third year. Nigerians are usually given a two-year visa, which you can renew after it expires if you leave the United States. However, as long as you are still a student, you are allowed to remain in the US even if the visa stamp expires. So when I heard about automatic visa revalidation, I was happy. Automatic visa revalidation allows you to travel with an expired visa to places like Canada, Mexico, or certain neighboring islands, as long as you do not stay longer than 30 days. You are then allowed to return to the US without any issues at customs. This was a big deal for me because at the time I could not travel back to Nigeria to renew my visa, but I really wanted to take an international trip since it had been a couple of years since I last travelled.

I checked the list of eligible countries, and St Kitts was on it. I booked my ticket and a short stay at an accommodation I found online. I printed all the information from the USCIS website, including the list of countries. The trip from Atlanta to St Kitts was about four hours. It is also important to mention that I did not need a visa to enter St Kitts with my Nigerian passport. It still feels strange to say that, because the Nigerian passport does not usually grant easy entry to most countries, but I pray that changes in the future.

When I arrived in St Kitts, my passport was stamped, and I took a bus to my accommodation. That was when my first bad experience started. The hotel had overbooked and did not have a room for me. The owner offered to take me to another place for one night and then bring me back to my original booking the next day, and I agreed. It was summer and extremely hot, but I was excited to go to the beach and relax. Transportation on the island was hard to navigate, and I ended up relying on the hotel owner or manager to drive me everywhere, which, in hindsight, I should not have done. I had five days in St. Kitts, and I wanted to make the most of it. My accommodation was in a very busy area, which made it easy to buy food and snacks, but the noise from cars and people made it hard to sleep or relax during the day.

On the second day, I went to the beach, listened to Caribbean music, and lay under the hot sun. It was a much-needed break from the constant work and pressure of being a PhD student. On the third day at the beach, I met a girl named Mango. She had the prettiest eyes. I remember her asking if I wanted to see a cool trick. Curious, I said yes. She told me her eyes could turn the color of the ocean. I watched her walk into the water and open her eyes, and immediately I realized she was right. Her eyes were exactly the color of the ocean. As a Nigerian, I can confidently say this would have scared my aunties, uncles, and my mom. She turned out to be an American who moved to St Kitts to study. We exchanged contacts and went our separate ways when the wind picked up. I also have to mention that the food on the island was amazing. Truly amazing.

Fast forward to the end of my trip. I arrived at the airport three hours before my flight, stood in line for Delta, and handed the agent my passport along with my documents explaining automatic visa revalidation and showing that St Kitts was included. I could immediately see the look on her face. The look of someone who had no idea what I was talking about. Fear took over my body. It was a familiar feeling, the feeling that this was about to be very hard to explain. I leaned forward and calmly explained why I was allowed to travel to St Kitts with an expired F-1 visa and return to the United States. She confirmed my fears and said, “I don’t know what you are talking about.” After about ten minutes of back and forth, she asked me to step aside while she attended to other customers. Part of me was relieved that I had arrived early. I thought no matter how long this took, I would still make my flight.

I was wrong. Two hours later, she still insisted she could not let me board because she had never heard of automatic visa revalidation. I called Delta Airlines directly to complain about the delay. The agent on the phone said she was not sure how to handle it. When I was transferred to a manager, the manager said she would make a note and guaranteed I would be able to fly the next day. Meanwhile, the agent at the counter kept trying to reach US immigration and stayed on hold for two hours without success. I kept showing her the printed USCIS information to prove what I was saying. I even suggested she check the USCIS website herself if she did not trust my printout, but none of it helped.

Eventually, my flight left without me. I had to book a new flight for the next day and find a hotel for the night, which I did. The next day, I arrived at the airport at 12 pm. My flight was scheduled for 5 pm. I told myself there was no way I would be left behind again.

I met a different agent this time. I explained everything to her. She also seemed confused, but the difference was that she immediately picked up the phone and tried to contact immigration. While waiting, I started searching for F-1 immigration lawyers who could help me. By this point, I was impatient, emotional, and exhausted from all the back and forth. I kept thinking that this would never have happened if I had gone to Mexico or Canada. It made sense that the agents had no prior experience with a situation like mine, since not many people travel to St Kitts using automatic visa revalidation.

Slowly, I watched the time pass. 12 pm became 1 pm, then 2 pm, then 3 pm, then 3:30 pm, then 4 pm. That was when everything hit me at once. I broke down and started crying in the airport. I was crying so hard that people around me thought someone had died. I was scared, and I felt completely alone. I kept asking myself what I would even do if I could not get back into the US.

I think seeing me like that pushed the agent to try even harder to reach immigration. I prayed for a last-minute miracle. At exactly 4:45 pm, immigration finally picked up and confirmed everything I had been saying for the past 24 hours. I was allowed to check in, and I was the last person to do so. I rushed to TSA and saw a long line of people ahead of me. I had less than ten minutes before the flight was set to leave.

Just as I stood at the back of the line, the agent who had helped me waved at me and asked me to come to the front. In that moment, I was incredibly grateful. I made it through TSA with four minutes to spare. If you have ever been to the St Kitts airport, you know you often have to walk on the tarmac to board the plane. I ran like my life depended on it. It felt like I was sprinting like Usain Bolt. I made it onto the plane, sat down, and finally let out a deep sigh of relief.

During the four-hour flight, I kept asking myself what the point of all of this was. All because I wanted to take a summer trip. When I landed in the US, customs took less than three minutes. They checked my passport, confirmed I had been in St Kitts for less than 30 days, and let me in.

I am sharing this story because so many people have questions about automatic visa revalidation. My advice is to travel to countries like Canada or Mexico, or to more popular destinations on the list, where airline agents are more familiar with the rules. Even better, call the airline ahead of time in your destination country and confirm that they understand automatic visa revalidation. It can save you a lot of stress.

Overall, I do look forward to visiting St. Kitts again, just not using automatic visa revalidation next time.
